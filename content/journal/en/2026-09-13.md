Today was less about shipping and more about listening to the product. No commits landed, but the session on `amarillo-v1` was useful in a different way: I spent it stress-testing the retrieval layer with the kind of questions a real user would actually ask.

### amarillo-v1

The prompts I threw at it were intentionally varied in shape: some were open-ended lookups ("names of the Lord"), some were thematic ("weight of glory"), some were tightly scoped to a passage ("context of 1Sm 6:5"), and one was reflective, closer to a devotional question than a factual one.

That mix matters. A Bible RAG has to handle concordance-style queries and interpretive ones without collapsing into the same generic answer. Watching how the system responds across that spectrum tells me more about the retrieval quality than any benchmark score would.

No code changed, but I came away with a mental list: which prompt shapes returned strong grounded answers, which ones drifted, and where the chunking or embedding strategy probably needs another pass. Days like this feel unproductive on the git log but usually shape the next few commits. The next session will likely turn these observations into concrete adjustments to the retrieval pipeline.
