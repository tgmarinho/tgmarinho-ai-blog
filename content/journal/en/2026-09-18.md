Today was less about shipping code and more about getting the domain right. Sometimes the highest-leverage work is spent reading vendor docs carefully until the vocabulary clicks.

### Financial domain mapping

I spent most of the day untangling how a third-party accounting API models its financial primitives. The core insight: in their vocabulary, a "lançamento" is not a clean synonym for accounts payable/receivable - it shows up on both sides, and the extract line is actually a separate entity with its own origin enum (sales, financial entries, transfers, tax payments, payroll, bank balance). That distinction matters because automatic bank reconciliation matches bank movements against already-registered payables and receivables using value and date rules. Once matched, the write-off happens automatically.

I committed two documents: a project master doc covering groups, financials, and the integration, plus a re-analyzed target schema with the open decisions still flagged - pricing, contact modeling, financial movement, and the connector itself. The real tension I want to sit with: who owns the source of truth? If our system duplicates suppliers, contacts, customers, and payables just to stay consistent, we start looking like a shadow copy of the vendor.

### Classifier as a product surface

Also sketched a proof of concept for slotting a scoring/classifier model into a support flow - ticket routing, refund/chargeback detection, and intent classification on inbound messages. Not text generation. Just: read context, pick from defined alternatives, return a score.
