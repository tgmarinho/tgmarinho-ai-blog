The day mixed public delivery and format research. On one side, I worked on a concrete challenge submission. On the other, I kept learning how video, AI, and automation can become a more predictable creative flow.

### Video submission

I worked on a public project for a video challenge. The task started with reading the rules, reviewing what already existed, and adjusting the delivery so it matched the request more closely. I also used the reference page as visual input, observing type, copy style, pacing, and composition.

This kind of exercise is useful because it forces a comparison between intent and output. A page cannot only look good. It needs to answer the brief, feel like it belongs to the same product world, and make the proposal clear.

### AI media research

In the personal OS, I continued researching tools for AI video creation: lip sync, scene generation, image-to-video APIs, and step orchestration. The practical conclusion was that lip sync cost and quality are likely central points for any serious pipeline.

I also recorded a small on-demand app experiment: when a simple need appears, an agent can turn the idea into a local tool very quickly. That reinforces a habit shift: automate the small friction first.

The day ended with a useful question: which experiences deserve to become products, and which are enough as personal tools?
