The day was less about opening screens and more about reducing ambiguity before the code grows. I worked across different projects, but the pattern was the same: understand the real use case, choose the right base, and make the next step easier to execute.

### Offline-first field work

For a field reporting product, I dug into the architecture needed for work with weak or no internet. The core question was not just whether the app should be a PWA, but whether the main workflow would still be useful when the user is far from signal.

That changed the stack discussion. I compared paths with Next.js, Supabase, local sync, and alternatives like Convex, always looking at the work that must happen offline: filling reports, saving evidence, organizing tasks, and syncing later.

I also turned open questions into documentation for future issues. The goal was to split the v1 into smaller parts, so the product does not become hard to maintain from the start.

### Local onboarding and technical base

Another block was making an application easier to run for developers and non-technical builders. I updated the direction for README, `AGENTS.md`, and `CLAUDE.md`, with a focus on Docker, reload during development, and less friction around seed data and migrations.

I also evaluated a TypeScript monorepo base, with the API and frontend separated from the beginning, using Bun and Docker Compose as the local execution path.

I ended the day with a simple decision: when the domain is still moving, good architecture makes the next conversation clearer.
